Section 03 — The Core Decision Engine

Selection Engine — All 16 Layers, In Full

This is the single heaviest engine in the entire platform, and the most important. It answers the two hardest questions Nxance faces: which exact instrument should the user buy, and how much of it. It runs a 16-layer analysis on every candidate across every asset class, combines all 16 into one composite FIT score, then makes the quantity decision separately. v1 had 12 layers; v2 adds 4 (Data Analysis, Own AI, Quantum Scoring, Human/Market Behaviour). The iron rule: no single layer decides. The FIT score is the collective verdict of all 16.

The 16 Layers — Every One, In Detail

How to read this: each candidate instrument passes through all 16 layers in parallel. Each layer returns a 0–100 score. The scores are combined with per-asset-class weights into one FIT score. Layer 8 (Fraud) is special — it's a hard veto, not a weighted score: fail it and the instrument is gone regardless of everything else.
🔬 Layers 1–4 · Instrument Quality
1 · Fundamental Analysis
Stocks/ETFs/REITs: P/E, ROE, debt-to-equity, free cash flow, revenue & earnings growth. FDs/bonds: issuer credit rating (CRISIL/ICRA), coupon vs prevailing market rate, duration risk. MFs: expense ratio, portfolio quality, AUM stability. The "is this fundamentally a good asset?" layer, computed differently per class.
2 · Technical Analysis
Applies where price-action matters — stocks, ETFs, commodities, futures, currency. Moving averages, RSI, volume trends, support/resistance. Deliberately near-zero weight for FDs (an FD has no meaningful chart). Timing and entry-point signal.
3 · Quant Finance
Factor exposure (value, growth, momentum, quality), risk-adjusted return ratios (Sharpe, Sortino, Treynor), beta vs benchmark, and alpha-generation history. This is the academic-grade "how does this behave as a statistical object?" layer.
4 · Instrument-Specific Deep Data
The layer that's different for literally every instrument type: fund-manager track record for MFs, promoter-holding changes for stocks, real yield for gold, occupancy & lease quality for REITs, roll-yield for commodities. Whatever is uniquely decisive for that instrument.
📉 Layers 5–8 · History & Risk
5 · Historical Performance
Full return history — not just the flattering recent stretch. 5-year, 10-year, and since-inception CAGR, each compared against the category average. Catches funds that look great on a 1-year view but chronically lag their peers over a full cycle.
6 · Crisis / Case Analysis
How did this specific instrument behave in 2008, the 2013 taper tantrum, 2018 IL&FS, the 2020 COVID crash, and the 2022 rate-hike cycle? Drawdown depth and recovery time in each. An instrument that fell 70% in 2008 and took 6 years to recover is scored very differently from one that fell 25% and recovered in 8 months.
7 · Risk Analysis (instrument-level)
Standalone volatility (standard deviation), downside deviation, Value-at-Risk, maximum drawdown — the instrument's own risk, before it ever enters portfolio-level risk (that's Sub-09's job). A high-return instrument with brutal standalone risk gets penalised here.
8 · Fraud Analysis — HARD VETO
SEBI/RBI action history, auditor red flags, promoter pledge levels, unusual related-party transactions, governance concerns. This is not a weighted score — it's a gate. Fail it and the instrument is rejected outright, no matter how brilliant every other layer looks. Great fundamentals never buy forgiveness for fraud risk.
🔮 Layers 9–12 · Forward-Looking + Fit
9 · News, Articles & Sentiment
NLP-based sentiment scoring on recent news and analyst coverage. NxanceLM reads and understands article sentiment — it's not keyword counting. A stock with strong fundamentals but a wave of credible negative coverage (regulatory probe, sector headwind) gets flagged before it's picked.
10 · Fresh Performance + Market Mood
The most recent quarter's numbers and the current market mood (a fear/greed-index equivalent). Catches what stale historical data misses — a fund that was excellent for 10 years but just had two bad quarters under a new manager, or a market that's currently euphoric and due for mean-reversion.
11 · Forecasting + Data Science
ML-based forward-return projection, per-instrument — the same Monte Carlo / time-series forecasting family used at the portfolio level, applied to each candidate. This is the "where is this likely to go?" layer, deliberately separated from Layer 13 which interrogates the past.
12 · User Behaviour Fit (volatility)
Does this specific instrument's volatility profile match what THIS user can actually tolerate (from Sub-01's stated behavioural score)? A mathematically optimal but wildly volatile instrument scores low for a nervous user even if it scores high for a calm one. Note: Layer 16 goes deeper on this — Layer 12 is the stated-tolerance check, Layer 16 is the observed-behaviour + crowd-psychology check.

Layers 13–16 — The Four New in v2, In Depth

🗄️ Layer 13 — Data Analysis POWERED BY SUB-19
What it does
A dedicated statistical layer that runs on the full raw dataset of every candidate — not summaries. Distribution analysis (is this fund's return skewed or fat-tailed?), regime detection (does it behave differently in high-inflation vs low-inflation periods?), anomaly detection (any suspicious NAV jumps?), liquidity-depth analysis (can the user actually exit at fair price?), and cross-sectional ranking against every peer in its category.
Why it's separate from Layer 11 (Forecasting)
Layer 11 predicts the future. Layer 13 interrogates the past and present at full statistical depth — it catches data-quality problems and structural oddities that a forecast model would silently absorb as noise.
🤖 Layer 14 — Own AI (NxanceLM) HELPER, NOT DICTATOR
What it does
NxanceLM reads what the other 15 layers cannot read: annual reports, concall transcripts, scheme documents, management commentary, regulatory filings — and cross-checks patterns across the trillions of data points in Sub-19's historical store. It then does two jobs: (1) produces its own 0–100 layer score, and (2) acts as a consistency checker — if Layer 1 says "excellent fundamentals" but Layer 9 sentiment is collapsing, it flags the contradiction and forces a deeper look before the composite is finalised.
How it helps quantity decisions
When deciding how much to hold, NxanceLM contributes context the math can't: "this AMC just lost its star fund manager", "this issuer's audit committee resigned" — soft signals that trim position size even when hard numbers still look fine.
Hard boundary
Its score is exactly one of 16 inputs, weighted like any other layer. It can flag, adjust, explain. It cannot override the collective verdict, and it can never push an instrument past a failed Fraud Gate. This is the "own AI is a parameter, not a dictator" rule made literal.
⚛️ Layer 15 — Quantum Scoring POWERED BY SUB-17
What it does
Classical layers score each candidate independently. Quantum scoring evaluates each candidate in the context of the whole portfolio being built — "how much better does the total portfolio become if this instrument enters it?" That's a combinatorial question (which subset of ~200 shortlisted instruments forms the best portfolio?), and combinatorial subset-selection is exactly the problem class quantum optimisation is designed for.
How, concretely
The shortlist is encoded as a QUBO — every instrument is a binary variable (in/out), expected returns from Layer 11 become the linear terms, pairwise correlations from Sub-05 become the quadratic terms. A QAOA circuit (GPU-simulated in Phase 1) samples low-energy solutions = high-quality subsets. Instruments appearing in many top solutions score high; ones that never appear score low, even if their standalone metrics look great.
Why this matters
This is the layer that catches "great instrument, wrong portfolio" — a top-ranked fund 0.95-correlated with three already-strong candidates scores poorly here, protecting real diversification the standalone layers would miss.
🧠 Layer 16 — Human / Market Behaviour POWERED BY SUB-16
What it does — two sides of behaviour
Market-side: behavioural-finance analysis of the crowd around this instrument — is it currently a herd-momentum favourite (retail inflow spikes, social hype) that history says tends to mean-revert painfully? User-side: deep behavioural match beyond Layer 12 — will THIS user, given their tracked archetype (Sub-16), actually hold this instrument through its normal drawdowns without panic-selling?
Difference from Layer 12
Layer 12 asks "does the volatility number match the user's stated tolerance?" Layer 16 asks "does the crowd psychology around this instrument, plus the user's observed behaviour, make this a hold-able position?" Stated and observed often disagree — Layer 16 trusts observed.

Combining the 16 — and the Separate Quantity Decision

# Composite FIT score — v2, 16 layers, one collective verdict
class SelectionEngineV2:
    """16 layers, weighted per asset class. No layer decides alone."""

    LAYER_WEIGHTS = {
      "stock": {"fundamental":0.14, "technical":0.07, "quant":0.10, "deep_data":0.05,
                "historical":0.06, "crisis":0.06, "risk":0.09, "fraud":0.07,
                "sentiment":0.05, "fresh":0.04, "forecast":0.07, "user_fit":0.05,
                "data_analysis":0.05, "nxancelm":0.04, "quantum":0.04, "behaviour":0.02},
      "fd":    {"fundamental":0.30, "risk":0.18, "fraud":0.14, "crisis":0.08,
                "forecast":0.08, "data_analysis":0.08, "nxancelm":0.06, "user_fit":0.08},
      # ... one weight profile per asset class (gold, REIT, MF, bond, ...)
      # technical ≈ 0 for FDs; crisis + behaviour weigh heavier for gold; etc.
    }

    def score_candidate(self, instrument, asset_class, user, portfolio_ctx):
        w = self.LAYER_WEIGHTS[asset_class]
        s = self.run_all_16_layers(instrument, user, portfolio_ctx)  # parallel
        if s["fraud"] < FRAUD_GATE:      # hard veto — nothing overrides this
            return REJECTED
        s = self.nxancelm_consistency_pass(s)  # flag cross-layer contradictions
        return sum(w[k] * s[k] for k in w)  # 0-100 collective FIT verdict

    def decide_quantity(self, ranked, slot_rs, corr_matrix, user):
        """WHICH = the FIT ranking above. HOW MUCH = this second pass."""
        # top-FIT names get bigger weight in the slot, BUT trimmed down if
        # highly correlated with each other (Sub-05) or if Layer-16 says this
        # user can't hold that much of this volatility profile.
        return weighted_allocation(ranked, slot_rs, corr_matrix,
                                    behaviour = user.archetype)
Two decisions, one engine
Which instrument = the 16-layer FIT ranking. How much quantity = a separate optimisation combining the FIT score, Sub-05 correlations, Layer-15's portfolio-context quantum score, and Layer-16's behavioural hold-ability. A top-ranked stock still gets a smaller slice if three other selected stocks already move the same way, or if this user can't stomach that much of it. Quantities are tuned to hit the user's required XIRR (Sub-01) at minimum risk — not to look mathematically pretty.

STEP 3 · DISTRIBUTE THE FUND · STAGE 1
First, distribute the amount across asset classes
Using the macro parameters against the user's expectation, the engine distributes the fund into a diversified blueprint: how much % (and how many ₹) go to each asset class — stocks, MF, FD, and the others live this phase. This is the allocation, done before any specific instrument is picked.
↓
STEP 4 · SELECTION · MACRO PARAMETER → SPECIFIC ASSET + QTY
Then pick the specific asset in each class and its quantity
For each allocation slot, the Selection Engine uses the macro selection parameters to shortlist, then the per-asset-class parameters (16 layers) to choose the specific instrument and decide its quantity — e.g. inside the equity slot, exactly which stocks/funds and how many units each.
↓
STEP 5 · ALL OTHER ENGINES
Shape the best diversified portfolio
Correlation (so slots don't secretly overlap), Risk, Forecasting, Crisis/backtest, Tax and Behaviour all contribute to make the blend optimal, not just a list of individually-good names.
↓
STEP 6 · OVERALL PARAMETER CHECK
Check the whole built portfolio against the expectation
The finished portfolio is run through the overall parameters as one whole to confirm it matches the user's expectation — required return, risk-fit, diversification, goal probability. If the overall check doesn't match, it loops back to distribution/selection (Step 3–4).
↓
STEP 7 · BACKTEST · THE GATE
Backtest the built portfolio vs the user's expectation
The portfolio is backtested across real history + the 5-crash set. PASS → send to user. FAIL → rebuild loop. Nothing that fails is shown.
↓
STEP 8 · SEND + EXPLAIN → EXECUTE
User reviews, with a reason for every number
On PASS, the portfolio goes to the user with a detailed explanation: why this much amount in this asset, why this instrument. The user can change anything — any change restarts the whole pipeline (re-distribute → re-select → re-check → re-backtest). If happy, they execute.
The Backend Number System — Everything Becomes a Score
To keep all of this fast and objective, the engine turns every single thing into a number behind the scenes. Every parameter, every layer, every instrument gets a score; the engine simply picks the highest-scoring options when constructing the portfolio. This is 100% backend — the user never sees raw numbers, only the plain-language result and its reason.
HOW SCORING WORKS (HIDDEN)
1 · Each macro parameter scores every asset 0–100 (Stage 1).
2 · Each per-asset-class parameter scores every instrument 0–100 within its own category (Stage 2, z-scored).
3 · Layers combine into one FIT score per instrument (weighted per class).
4 · The Fraud gate can zero out any score (hard veto).
5 · The engine ranks by score, picks the best; quantity is a second scored pass (correlation + behaviour trim).
6 · The whole result gets a backtest score — the pass/fail gate before the user sees anything.

Front-end (shown): only the outcome in plain language — "this holding drags you down because X; the better fit is Y; here's how much; here's why." No raw scores, no formulas on screen.

Sub-Engines 01–15 — Every Engine, Full Detail

Every one of the fifteen v1 sub-engines is documented here in full — trigger, inputs, method, output, and how it connects — so this document stands completely on its own. In v2 each engine also reports its intermediate output up to the Supervision ring (16/17/18), and Sub-01 gains behaviour capture. 📋 SUB-01 · Profile & Goal Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE
Trigger
User taps "Create Portfolio" and answers 6–8 questions. This is the very first engine in the pipeline — everything downstream depends on it.
Inputs (the questions)
Goal type (retirement / house / education / wealth), target amount, timeline, current savings, monthly investable amount, income stability, past investing experience, and a hypothetical "your portfolio drops 20% tomorrow — what do you do?" scenario.
Output 1 — Risk Score (0–100)
A composite of stated risk comfort + implied risk from income stability (a stable govt salary can afford more equity than volatile freelance income) + reaction to the loss scenario. These three rarely agree, and the blend is more honest than any single answer.
Output 2 — Required XIRR
Solved backward from target amount, timeline and current savings using the future-value formula. This is the single most important number in the whole engine — it's the return the portfolio must achieve, and everything downstream optimises toward it.
v2 addition — behaviour capture
Sub-01 now also records implicit signals for Sub-16: how fast Priya answers, which answers she revises, how long she hesitates on the loss question. Stated words + implicit hesitation together are the first seed of her behaviour archetype.

Where it hands off: Risk Score → Sub-16 (behaviour adjust) → Sub-02 (allocation). Required XIRR → Sub-09 (the target risk must respect) and every optimiser.
⚖️ SUB-02 · Asset Allocation Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE + ON REDESIGN
What it decides
The top-level % split across asset classes — before any specific instrument is chosen. "How much equity vs debt vs gold vs REITs" is a fundamentally different question from "which stock", and it's settled first.
Method — Black-Litterman
It combines market-implied equilibrium returns (what the whole market currently expects from each class) with the user's specific risk score and timeline. This is better than naive fixed templates because it starts from what the market actually prices, then tilts for the individual.
Asset classes it splits across
Equity (stocks/ETF), Debt (bonds/FD), Gold, Real Estate (REITs), Commodities, Currency, Alternatives (startup/PE) — but which classes are even available is set by Sub-20/21 (India vs global, amount-unlock).
Example output
A 28-yr-old, 15-yr horizon, high tolerance → 70% equity / 15% debt / 10% gold / 5% alternatives. A 55-yr-old, 3-yr horizon → flips almost entirely to debt + gold. Priya (score 54 after behaviour adjust, 12yr) → 58/22/12/8.
v2 change
Receives the behaviour-adjusted risk score from Sub-16, not the raw Sub-01 score — so a stated-aggressive but observed-nervous user gets a gentler split from the very first step.
Note: Sub-03 (Selection Engine) is the heaviest engine in the platform and gets its own full section next (Section 03 — the 16 layers). Here we continue with the engines around it.
🛡️ SUB-04 · Constraint + Fraud Screening BUILD · RUNS ONCE
What it does
Encodes the user's hard rules — ESG preferences, sector exclusions ("no tobacco"), liquidity needs — into a formal constraint set the optimiser must respect. And it runs the Fraud Gate: SEBI/RBI action history, auditor red flags, promoter pledge levels.
Why it runs BEFORE Selection
It pre-filters the candidate universe. No point running the expensive 16-layer analysis on an instrument that fails a hard exclusion or a fraud check anyway. Shrinking ~2,000 candidates to a few hundred here is a big part of how the <10s budget is met.
Fraud = hard veto
Fraud is never a "scored factor" that good fundamentals can outweigh. It's a gate: fail it and the instrument is gone, no matter how attractive everything else looks. This philosophy repeats throughout the engine.
📈 SUB-05 · Cross-Asset Correlation Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE + ON REBALANCE
What it computes
Correlation not just within equity, but across equity / debt / gold / real-estate / commodities / currency. This is the difference between real diversification and fake diversification — owning 20 stocks that all crash together is not diversified; owning assets that move differently is.
Where it feeds
Directly into Selection's quantity decision (a great instrument gets a smaller slice if it's highly correlated with picks you already have) and into the optimisers (Sub-06/07). In v2 the correlation matrix also becomes the quadratic terms of the QUBO for Layer-15 quantum scoring.
Method
Rolling covariance matrices computed in Rust (fast, done often), with shrinkage estimators to stay stable when data is thin — raw sample correlations are noisy and can mislead the optimiser.
📊 SUB-06 · MVO Optimisation Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE + ON REBALANCE
Method
Classical Modern Portfolio Theory mean-variance optimisation — for a set of selected instruments, find the weights that maximise expected return per unit of risk (the highest-Sharpe point on the efficient frontier).
Why it exists alongside quantum
It is the always-works baseline and the fallback. MVO is well-understood, fast, and never fails weirdly. Quantum (Sub-07) has to beat this baseline to be used — if QAOA times out or produces a worse Sharpe, MVO's answer ships and the user never notices. This is the safety net under the whole optimisation chain.
Output
A first-pass optimal weight for every selected instrument. These weights then go head-to-head against Sub-07's quantum weights.
⚡ SUB-07 · QAOA Quantum Optimisation Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE + ON REBALANCE
Method
A quantum-classical hybrid. The portfolio weighting problem — especially with realistic messy constraints (integer lots, minimum ticket sizes, "hold at most 15 names") — is encoded as a QUBO matrix and solved with a QAOA circuit (GPU-simulated in Phase 1, real QPU in Full Final). Full mechanics are in Section 11 (Quantum, End to End).
The shoot-out rule
Sub-07's result is always compared against Sub-06's MVO baseline on real numbers (Sharpe, VaR, constraint satisfaction). Better Sharpe wins. Quantum is never trusted just because it's newer — it earns its place every single build or it doesn't ship.
v2 change
Now coordinated by Q-Core (Sub-17) and shares the same QUBO infrastructure — the weight-optimisation QUBO here is a smaller cousin of the global-optimality QUBO Q-Core runs over the whole portfolio.
🕒 SUB-08 · Backtesting Engine BUILD · RUNS ONCE + VALIDATION LOOP
What it proves
That the constructed portfolio would have survived real crashes — not a smooth theoretical model, but the actual historical hell of: 2008 Global Financial Crisis, 2013 taper tantrum, 2018 IL&FS, 2020 COVID crash, 2022 rate-hike cycle. It replays the exact portfolio through each period.
What it reports
Maximum drawdown (how deep did it fall), time-to-recovery (how long to climb back), and whether it stayed inside the user's stated risk tolerance during each crisis.
The feedback loop — not a one-way check
If the portfolio fails a crash test, it goes back to Sub-06/07 for re-optimisation with tighter risk constraints. This can loop a few times. It's a real gate, not a rubber stamp.
v2 addition — behavioural backtest
Beyond "did the portfolio survive", it now asks "would THIS user have held it?" A −40% drawdown a portfolio mathematically recovers from is useless if a Panic-Prone user would have sold at the bottom. For Priya it caught a borderline case and nudged 3% more into gold.
⚠️ SUB-09 · Risk Analysis Engine BUILD + MANAGE · CONTINUOUS
The philosophy — risk is the boundary, not an afterthought
Most tools optimise for return first and mention risk later. Nxance inverts this. Risk tolerance is the hard boundary; return is only ever whatever is achievable inside it. We never chase a bigger number by quietly taking more risk than the user can handle.
What it enforces
Portfolio-level volatility, Value-at-Risk (VaR), and maximum-drawdown ceilings derived from the user's risk score. Any proposed portfolio that breaches them is rejected before the user ever sees it.
Finds the minimum
Among all portfolios that hit the required XIRR, it selects the one with the least risk. This is literally the "minimum risk" clause of the motive, made into code.
v2 change
Q-Core (Sub-17) independently re-verifies its VaR numbers using quantum-amplitude-estimation-style Monte Carlo. If the two disagree beyond tolerance, the portfolio is not released until resolved — two independent risk checks, not one.
Runs forever
Not just at build — it keeps checking during the Manage phase, so market drift can never quietly push the user past their risk limit without something catching it.
💰 SUB-10 · Tax-Aware Optimiser BUILD · FINAL STEP + ON REBALANCE
What it does
Takes the QAOA/MVO-optimised weights and makes small adjustments to minimise expected future tax drag — LTCG/STCG-aware, prefers instruments with favourable tax treatment (e.g. equity held >1yr, indexation on debt) when two options are otherwise similar in FIT score.
The trade-off it manages carefully
It never sacrifices meaningful Sharpe for tax savings. These are nudges at the margin, not a redesign. A slightly worse-taxed instrument that's clearly better on FIT still wins. Tax is a tiebreaker, not a driver.
Also runs on rebalance
When Sub-12 rebalances, it calls Sub-10 again — so drift-correction never triggers unnecessary short-term capital gains just to hit a perfect target number.

The Manage Layer — Engines 11–15, Running for Years

A portfolio built perfectly on Day 1 must stay well-built on Day 1,000. These five engines run for the life of the portfolio. The rule that governs all of them: they observe and propose, they never trade silently — every real-money action is explained and needs user approval.

👀 SUB-11 · Analysis & Managing Engine MANAGE · DAILY
The daily heartbeat
Runs every single day, whether or not anything needs to change. It's the engine that's always watching.
What it checks daily
Weight drift vs target for every instrument, portfolio performance vs benchmark, and any single instrument whose Selection score suddenly drops (e.g. a held stock that just triggered a fraud flag or a ratings downgrade).
Does NOT decide alone
It only observes and flags. When a threshold is crossed it hands off to Sub-12 (Rebalance), Sub-13 (Redesign), or Sub-15 (Life-Event). It never silently trades on its own — a critical trust property.
v2 change
Feeds its daily digest up to Sub-18 (NxanceLM), which turns raw telemetry into the plain-language "how is my SIP doing" screen and the periodic report cards.
⚖️ SUB-12 · Rebalancing Engine MANAGE · EVENT-DRIVEN
What triggers it
Drift: an asset class weight moves >5% from target (equity meant to be 58% grew to 66% after a rally). Instrument failure: a held name's Selection score drops sharply — immediate review regardless of drift. Time-based: a periodic review re-runs Selection to check if better instruments have emerged.
What it actually does
Computes the exact sell/buy amounts to return to target allocation, calls Sub-10 to stay tax-aware (never triggers needless STCG), and proposes the rebalance — explained in plain language by NxanceLM — which the user approves before Sub-14 executes.
v2 change — behaviour-adapted
The rebalance style now adapts to the archetype. A Panic-Prone user gets calm, context-first framing and gentler thresholds; a Disciplined user gets the raw numbers and tighter thresholds because they'll actually confirm on time. Same math, delivery tuned to keep the user invested.
🔄 SUB-13 · Redesigning Engine (SIP) MANAGE · MONTHLY + ON-DEMAND
Different job from rebalancing
Rebalancing fixes an existing portfolio drifting. Redesigning handles the SIP itself changing — the monthly contribution and any change to its amount.
Trigger 1 — monthly cycle
Runs on the SIP date. Usually just executes the same plan at current prices; redesign logic only kicks in if NAVs/prices have moved enough to change which instrument should get this month's contribution.
Trigger 2 — user changes amount
Increase: re-runs Sub-02 at the new amount — a larger SIP can unlock higher-minimum instruments (via Sub-21). Decrease: recomputes required XIRR (Sub-01); if the lower amount can no longer hit the goal on time, NxanceLM says so honestly and offers a revised timeline or target.
✅ SUB-14 · Execution & Order Engine MANAGE · EVERY ACTION
What it does
The only engine that touches real money. Converts every approved decision — build, rebalance, redesign — into actual SIP mandates, buy/sell orders, and payment triggers.
Talks to
The Payments service (Go) for SIP mandate setup, Razorpay for money movement, BSE StAR MF / exchange / broker APIs for orders, and the Notification service to confirm every executed action to the user.
The hard rule — user approval, always
Nothing here fires without explicit user approval for the specific action. It's built as an idempotent state machine with a full audit log — critical both for reliability and for SEBI compliance. This is the engine that makes "easy" never mean "automatic without consent."
🎯 SUB-15 · Life-Event Adaptation Engine MANAGE · EVENT-DRIVEN
What it does
Detects signals of a major life change — marriage, new child, job loss, a new goal added, a sudden windfall — and triggers a full re-profile through Sub-01, rather than waiting for gradual drift to slowly reveal the mismatch.
Why it's needed — the gap it closes
Rebalancing fixes drift within an unchanged goal. Redesigning fixes SIP amount within an unchanged goal. Neither handles "the goal itself changed." A new baby changes everything about a plan — this engine is the only one that catches that and restarts the profiling.
Signals it watches
Explicit user updates (new goal added in-app), plus inferred signals from activity patterns and SIP behaviour — always confirmed with the user before a re-profile, never assumed.
The Manage layer in one line
Sub-11 watches, Sub-12 fixes drift, Sub-13 handles the SIP, Sub-15 catches life changes — and Sub-14 is the single hand that touches money, only ever after you say yes. Together they're the reason a good Day-1 portfolio is still a good portfolio a decade later.

At onboarding (stated + implicit)

Section 06 — New Sub-Engine

SUB-17 · Quantum Supervisor Engine (Q-Core)

A dedicated quantum sub-engine whose job is to monitor the entire Construction Engine — every stage, every intermediate output — and verify that the final result meets the user's expectations with minimum risk, in the minimum time period, while making the whole process easier. Sub-07 (QAOA) optimises weights at one step; Q-Core supervises the whole pipeline.

⚛️ Job 1 — Global Optimality Check
The build pipeline makes decisions stage-by-stage (allocation → selection → weights). Stage-wise decisions can be locally right but globally sub-optimal. Q-Core re-encodes the entire final portfolio as one big QUBO — instruments, quantities, correlations, risk constraints, required XIRR, and the user's behaviour constraints together — and searches the full combinatorial space for any configuration that hits the same target with less risk or less time. If it finds one that's meaningfully better, the pipeline re-runs with Q-Core's hint. If not, the portfolio is certified.
🎲 Job 2 — Quantum Monte Carlo Risk Verification
Independently re-verifies Sub-09's risk numbers using quantum-amplitude-estimation-style Monte Carlo (simulated in Phase 1). Classical Monte Carlo needs ~1M scenario paths for tight VaR confidence; amplitude estimation reaches the same confidence quadratically faster — which is what makes a heavy risk re-check affordable inside a 10-second budget. If Q-Core's VaR disagrees with Sub-09's by more than tolerance, the portfolio is not released until resolved.
⏱️ Job 3 — Minimum-Time Path Search
Users don't just want the goal — they want it as early as safely possible. Q-Core runs the goal as a multi-period optimisation: across thousands of SIP-step/allocation-glide-path combinations, which one reaches the target amount earliest without ever breaching the user's risk boundary? Multi-period path problems explode combinatorially — again exactly the problem shape quantum sampling handles well.
🧭 Job 4 — Process Simplification Monitor
Watches the pipeline itself: which stages take longest, which re-run loops (Backtest→re-optimise) fire most often, where users drop off in the questionnaire. Produces optimisation hints for Sub-20 (speed) and product hints for the team ("question 6 causes 30% of hesitation — reword it"). Supervision includes making the machine simpler, not just the portfolio better.
⚖️ Q-Core also obeys the Golden Rule
Q-Core verifies and proposes — it never overrides. Its "better configuration found" output goes back through the same collective pipeline (Selection → Risk → Backtest) before anything changes. A quantum result that can't survive the classical checks is discarded. And every Q-Core computation runs with a classical fallback — if the quantum path times out or misbehaves, MVO + classical Monte Carlo results stand, and the build still completes in budget.

Section 09 — New Sub-Engine · SUB-23

SUB-23 · Asset Universe & Eligibility Engine — India, Global & Amount-Unlock

Before Selection can choose anything, something must decide which assets even exist for this user. Three rules govern the universe: a sensible country default, a user override for global exposure, and a hard affordability gate that unlocks high-ticket classes only when the user can genuinely sustain them. This is what keeps a ₹5,000-SIP user out of illiquid real estate and a ₹50-lakh user from missing it.

🇮🇳 Rule 1 — Country Default
By default the universe is the user's own country's available assets — for India that means mainly stocks, mutual funds, FDs and bonds (plus ETFs and REITs). This is the safe, familiar, tax-simple base every user starts from. No user has to understand global markets to get a complete portfolio.
🌐 Rule 2 — Global Opt-In
At the questionnaire the user chooses India-only or global. Pick global and the universe expands to world equities, international ETFs and global bonds — all still run through the same 16-layer Selection and the same risk boundary. Nothing is added just because it's foreign; it competes on FIT like everything else.
💱 Rule 3 — Forex & Commodities
Forex and commodities are treated as globally uniform — gold is gold, crude is crude, the world over. So they're suggested from the overall global market automatically (no country selection needed); only the transaction currency differs. The user never picks a "country" for these — the engine just adds them when they serve the goal.
🔓 SUB-23's Universe Gate — Amount-Based Asset Unlock AFFORDABILITY-CHECKED
The default base (everyone)
Stocks, MF, FD, bonds, ETF, currency and forex form the base portfolio for every user regardless of ticket size. This alone builds a genuinely diversified portfolio for a ₹500/month SIP.
High-ticket classes (gated)

The check verifies the user can actually sustain the commitment alongside their goal — not just that one number crossed a threshold. A user with a ₹6-lakh windfall but no stable income does not get pushed into illiquid PE.

Section 11 — The Quant Core

Formulas, Algorithms & Math — Engine by Engine

Every number the Construction Engine produces comes from a formula on this page. This is the exact math each sub-engine runs — the same reference Raghav codes against and the same math an investor's technical due-diligence will test. Where a formula has a choice (parametric vs historical VaR, etc.), the production choice is stated. 📋 A · SUB-01 — Goal & Profile Math
A1 · Future Value of the SIP (monthly, invested at month-start)
FVSIP = P × [ ((1+i)n − 1) / i ] × (1+i)
P = monthly SIP amount  ·  i = expected monthly return = (1+XIRR)1/12 − 1  ·  n = number of months
A2 · Total corpus (existing investments + SIP)
FVtotal = C₀ × (1+r)T + FVSIP
C₀ = current savings/investments moved into the plan  ·  r = annual XIRR  ·  T = years to goal
A3 · Required XIRR — the most important number in the engine
Solve for r:  C₀(1+r)T + P·[((1+i)12T − 1)/i]·(1+i) = Goal  ,  i = (1+r)1/12 − 1
No closed form → solved numerically by bisection (guaranteed convergence, ~40 iterations to 0.01% precision, microseconds in Rust):
def required_xirr(goal, C0, P, T, lo=0.0, hi=0.60):
    for _ in range(60):                      # bisection
        r = (lo + hi) / 2
        if fv_total(C0, P, T, r) < goal: lo = r
        else: hi = r
    if r > MAX_SAFE_XIRR[risk_profile]:       # e.g. 0.15 for conservative
        return UNREACHABLE_SAFELY                # → honest conversation, not silent risk
    return r
A4 · Composite Risk Score (0–100)
RS = 0.40·Sstated + 0.25·Scapacity + 0.20·Sloss-reaction + 0.15·Sexperience
Behaviour adjustment:  RS′ = RS − λ · Bgap  ,  λ ≈ 0.5, Bgap = |stated − observed behaviour score|
Scapacity comes from income stability + surplus ratio (investable ÷ income)  ·  Bgap from Sub-16 (hesitation, revisions, red-day telemetry). Priya in the worked example: RS 62 → RS′ 54.
⚖️ B · SUB-02 — Black-Litterman Allocation
B1 · Market-implied equilibrium returns (the neutral starting point)
Π = δ · Σ · wmkt
δ = market risk-aversion coefficient  ·  Σ = asset-class covariance matrix (from Sub-05)  ·  wmkt = market-cap weights. This says: "before any opinion, what does the market itself imply each class returns?"
B2 · Posterior expected returns (market + Nxance views combined)
E[R] = [ (τΣ)⁻¹ + Pᵀ Ω⁻¹ P ]⁻¹ · [ (τΣ)⁻¹ Π + Pᵀ Ω⁻¹ Q ]
P = view matrix (which classes each view touches)  ·  Q = the views themselves (from Layer-11 forecasts, e.g. "equity to beat debt by 4%")  ·  Ω = view-uncertainty matrix (low-confidence forecasts get wide Ω → pulled toward market)  ·  τ ≈ 0.05 scaling. Why BL and not raw MVO here: raw mean-variance at asset-class level over-reacts violently to small return-estimate errors; BL anchors to market equilibrium and bends only as far as forecast confidence justifies.
B3 · Risk-score → equity glide (the personalisation)
wequity = wBL-equity × f(RS′, T)  ,  f = min(1, (RS′/100)·(0.6 + 0.4·min(T,15)/15))
Longer horizon + higher behaviour-adjusted risk score → closer to the full BL equity weight; short horizon or low RS′ shifts the surplus to debt/gold. The exact f() is a tunable engine parameter, audited via the Rejection Log.
🎯 C · SUB-03 — Selection Math: FIT Score, Layers & Quantity
C1 · Layer-score normalisation (every raw metric → 0–100)
z = (x − μcategory) / σcategory  →  s = 100 · Φ(z)
Every metric is z-scored against its own category (a small-cap fund vs small-cap funds, an AAA bond vs AAA bonds), then mapped through the normal CDF Φ so scores are comparable across layers. Cross-category comparison without this step is how naive rankers go wrong.
C2 · The Composite FIT Score — the collective verdict
FIT = Σℓ=1..16 wℓ,class · sℓ   subject to   sfraud ≥ gate (else REJECTED)  ,  Σwℓ = 1
Weights wℓ,class differ per asset class (weights table in Section 03). Fraud is a constraint, not a weighted term — no score can average away a fraud failure.
C3 · Key technical-layer formulas (Layer 2)
RSI:  RSI = 100 − 100/(1 + avg gain₁₄/avg loss₁₄)
EMA:  EMAt = α·Pricet + (1−α)·EMAt−1 ,  α = 2/(N+1)
Momentum (12-1):  M = Pt−21/Pt−252 − 1
C4 · Quant-layer ratios (Layer 3)
Sharpe: (Rp − Rf)/σp  ·  Sortino: (Rp − Rf)/σdown  ·  Treynor: (Rp − Rf)/β
Beta: β = Cov(Ri, Rm)/Var(Rm)  ·  Alpha (CAPM): α = Ri − [Rf + β(Rm − Rf)]
Information Ratio: IR = (Rp − Rbench)/TrackingError
C5 · Quantity decision (which is separate from ranking)
Raw tilt:  qi ∝ FITi × (1 − ρ̄i) × Hi  → normalised: wi = qi/Σqj, capped wi ≤ capclass
ρ̄i = mean correlation of candidate i with instruments already selected (from Sub-05)  ·  Hi = behavioural hold-ability multiplier from Layer 16 (0.7–1.0). A top-FIT stock still gets a smaller slice if three selected stocks already move with it. These raw weights then go to MVO/QAOA as the starting point — the optimisers refine them against the full risk constraint set.
🔮 D · Layer 11 + Goal Probability — Forecasting & Monte Carlo
D1 · Price-path model (Geometric Brownian Motion baseline)
St = S₀ · exp( (μ − σ²/2)·t + σ·Wt )
μ = expected drift (from the ML forecast, not naive historical mean)  ·  σ = volatility  ·  Wt = Wiener process (random walk). Production uses GBM + fat-tail correction (Student-t innovations, df≈5) because Indian equity returns have fatter tails than normal — pure GBM understates crash risk.
D2 · ML forecast (the μ that feeds D1)
Gradient boosting objective:  L = Σ loss(yi, ŷi) + Σ Ω(fk)  — XGBoost/LightGBM on features: trailing returns, valuation gap, macro (rates, inflation, INR), flows
Every forecast ships with a confidence interval; a point forecast without a band is treated as noise. Low-confidence forecasts widen Ω in Black-Litterman (B2) — the math automatically listens less to shaky predictions.
D3 · Probability of reaching the goal (the number the user sees)
P(goal) = (1/N) · Σk=1..N 𝟙[ FVk ≥ Goal ]  ,  N = 10,000 simulated paths of the full portfolio + SIP schedule
Re-computed after every market day by Sub-18's tracking ledger; independently re-verified by Q-Core's amplitude-estimation Monte Carlo (H2 below) at build time.
📈 E · SUB-05 — Correlation & Portfolio Variance
E1 · Pairwise correlation (rolling, crisis-aware)
ρij = Cov(Ri, Rj) / (σi · σj)  ,  computed on 250-day rolling windows and separately on crisis windows only
The crisis-window matrix matters more: correlations that look low in calm markets spike toward 1 in crashes — the engine diversifies against the crisis matrix, not the calm one. Covariance shrinkage (Ledoit-Wolf) is applied so the matrix stays stable when history is short.
E2 · Portfolio variance — the quantity everything minimises
σp² = wᵀ Σ w = Σi Σj wi wj σi σj ρij
📊 F · SUB-06 — Mean-Variance Optimisation (the classical baseline)
minw   wᵀΣw   subject to:   wᵀμ ≥ rreq ,  Σwi = 1 ,  0 ≤ wi ≤ capi ,  class bounds from SUB-02
Note the direction: Nxance's MVO minimises risk subject to the required return — not maximises return subject to risk. That is Sub-09's philosophy encoded straight into the objective. Solved as a quadratic program via CVXPY (OSQP solver); with integer-lot and cardinality constraints added, the problem becomes non-convex → handed to QUBO/QAOA (H below).
⚠️ G · SUB-09 — Risk Mathematics
G1 · Value-at-Risk (95% / 99%)
Parametric:  VaRα = −(μp − zα·σp) × V  ·  Historical:  VaRα = −quantile1−α(historical P&L)
Production reports the worse of the two — parametric is fast but assumes normality; historical captures the fat tails. z₀.₉₅ = 1.645, z₀.₉₉ = 2.326, V = portfolio value.
G2 · Conditional VaR (expected shortfall — the honest tail number)
CVaRα = E[ L | L ≥ VaRα ]
G3 · Maximum Drawdown & Downside Deviation
MDD = maxt ( (Peakt − Vt) / Peakt )  ·  σdown = √( E[ min(R − MAR, 0)² ] ) , MAR = required monthly return
G4 · The engine's core optimisation — minimum risk for the target
minw CVaR95(w)   s.t.   XIRRexpected(w) ≥ rreq ,  MDDbacktest(w) ≤ MDDtolerance(RS′)
Required XIRR is the constraint; risk is the objective. If the feasible set is empty (target unreachable within the drawdown tolerance), the engine reports honestly instead of silently relaxing the risk bound — that branch is hard-coded, not a judgment call.
⚛️ H · SUB-07 / SUB-17 — Quantum Mathematics (QUBO · Ising · QAOA · QAE)
H1 · The QUBO — every quantum problem in the engine reduces to this
minx∈{0,1}ⁿ   xᵀQx  =  Σi Qiixi + Σi<j Qijxixj
xi = 1 if instrument i is selected (or trade i executed, or path i chosen)  ·  Qii = −μi (expected return, negated because we minimise)  ·  Qij = λ·σiσjρij (risk/correlation penalty)  ·  constraints enter as penalties: pick-exactly-K → add A·(Σxi − K)², budget → add A·(Σcixi − B)², with A large enough that violating a constraint always costs more than any return gain.
H2 · QUBO → Ising (what the quantum hardware actually solves)
Substitute si = 2xi − 1 ∈ {−1,+1}  →  HC = Σ hisi + Σi<j Jijsisj
The portfolio problem becomes finding the ground state (lowest energy) of a spin system — which is exactly what annealers and QAOA circuits are built to approximate.
H3 · QAOA — the circuit
|ψ(γ,β)⟩ = Πk=1..p e−iβkHM · e−iγkHC |+⟩⊗n  ,  minimise  ⟨ψ(γ,β)| HC |ψ(γ,β)⟩ over (γ,β)
HC = cost Hamiltonian (the portfolio problem)  ·  HM = mixer (drives exploration)  ·  p = 2–4 layers in production  ·  the 2p angles (γ,β) are tuned by a classical optimiser (COBYLA/SPSA) between circuit runs — this loop is why it's called hybrid. Measuring the final state many times yields a distribution of candidate portfolios; the best measured bitstrings go to the classical post-check.
H4 · Quantum Amplitude Estimation — why Q-Core's risk re-check is affordable
Classical Monte Carlo error:  ε ∼ O(1/√N)   ·   QAE error:  ε ∼ O(1/N)  — quadratic speed-up in samples for the same VaR confidence
1M classical paths ≈ 1k quantum-amplified queries for the same precision. Phase 1 runs the QAE-style estimator on the GPU simulator; the speed-up becomes physical when real QPUs mature — the code path is identical either way.

🔧 How It's Built — the Hybrid Loop, Step by Step
  • Step 1 — Formulate as QUBO. Every quantum use-case above reduces to: minimise x'Qx where x is a binary vector (instrument in/out, trade yes/no, path chosen/not). Expected returns fill the diagonal of Q; correlations and penalty terms (risk boundary, budget, cardinality) fill the off-diagonal. This formulation step is 80% of the real engineering work — and it's pure classical Python.
  • Step 2 — Sample with QAOA. The QUBO maps to an Ising Hamiltonian; a parameterised QAOA circuit (typically p=2–4 layers) is run many times; a classical optimiser (COBYLA/SPSA) tunes the circuit parameters between runs. Output: a distribution of candidate solutions, best ones kept.
  • Step 3 — Classical post-check. Every quantum-proposed solution is re-evaluated exactly, classically (Sharpe, VaR, constraints). Only solutions that beat the classical baseline on real numbers survive. Quantum proposes, classical verifies — always.
  • Step 4 — Fallback guaranteed. Timeout, noisy result, or service failure → MVO + classical Monte Carlo answer ships. The user never waits on quantum, and never receives a quantum-only, unverified result.
