Executive Summary
Nxance is an AI-powered investment intelligence platform built by Ohshn Intelligence LLP to answer a question that no existing product answers with full transparency: does an investor's portfolio actually work, and if not, exactly how should it be fixed. The platform is deliberately positioned as an intelligence company, not an advisory company — it produces quantitatively grounded, explainable analysis rather than discretionary recommendations, and every figure it shows a user is traceable to a deterministic calculation rather than a language model's guess.
The platform is built around three engines that operate together rather than independently: a Portfolio Health Check engine that diagnoses an investor's existing holdings across a 12-layer quantitative analysis framework; a Portfolio Construction engine that builds a new portfolio from a goal-driven questionnaire using mean-variance optimisation; and NxanceLM, Nxance's AI layer, built on top of a licensed third-party large language model rather than a model Nxance trains itself, which performs research and analysis inside both engines, generates every explanation and report, and answers finance and economy questions through a dedicated terminal interface. Nxance's own in-house-built and -trained intellectual property is the Health Check engine, the Construction engine, and the terminal application layer; the underlying language model inside NxanceLM is licensed, not developed by Nxance. A proprietary control layer named Number Guard sits underneath all three, verifying that any number NxanceLM shows a user was actually computed by a deterministic engine — structurally eliminating hallucinated figures rather than merely discouraging them.
The opportunity sits inside two converging trends. First, retail participation in capital markets is compounding rapidly in India and other emerging economies, while advisory penetration of that base remains structurally low. Second, the global market for AI-driven wealth-management software is itself in a period of rapid build-out, with independent research houses estimating the global WealthTech solutions market at roughly USD 7–24 billion in 2026, expanding toward the tens of billions by the early 2030s as artificial intelligence, cloud infrastructure and open-finance regulation combine to modernise the industry.
Nxance's business model is a two-tier subscription — a full Health Check + Construction + NxanceLM tier and an NxanceLM-only tier — sold both business-to-consumer in India first and, on a phased basis, business-to-business to boutique advisory firms, registered investment advisers, family offices and institutions as a white-labelled intelligence layer. The company's near-term roadmap (Era 1) is deliberately scoped to proving the retail app end-to-end before any institutional, deep-technology or international expansion (Era 2 and Era 3) is pursued.
The company's current authoritative financial ask, drawn from its September 2026 financial model, is approximately ₹12.49 crore (₹1,248.70 lakh) to fund eighteen months of fully-staffed operations, inclusive of a 12% contingency and a SEBI Registered Investment Adviser deposit tracked separately. This report consolidates Nxance's market research, competitive positioning, technical architecture, regulatory posture, business model, financial plan and long-term vision into a single reference document, structured in the format of an institutional research report, for use with investors, grant committees, and internal strategic planning.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement
1.1 The Global Problem
Across both emerging and developed economies, the number of people who hold investable assets has grown far faster than the number of people who have access to rigorous, ongoing, and transparent analysis of what those assets are actually doing for them. Two failure modes recur globally: investors who assemble a portfolio piecemeal — through employer schemes, social-media prompts, family advice, or self-directed apps — and never learn whether the resulting combination is efficient, overlapping, overexposed, or overpriced; and investors who receive advice from a human or automated adviser that they are structurally unable to independently verify, because the reasoning behind the recommendation is opaque.
Traditional wealth advisory does not scale to the size of this population because it is built around human relationship-driven service; low-cost robo-advisory scales but has historically done so by trading away explainability, offering a recommended allocation without a defensible, inspectable reason for every component of it. Nxance's founding thesis is that this trade-off is not necessary: a quantitative engine can be both scaled and explainable, if the explanation layer is architecturally prevented from inventing what it says.
1.2 India as the Initial Proving Ground
India is the platform's first market because it currently exhibits the clearest version of this gap at scale. Retail participation in Indian capital markets has grown sharply: SEBI's monthly bulletin recorded the country's total demat account base at roughly 25.7 crore by June 2026, and separate NSDL/CDSL/SEBI data placed the account count above 237 million by August 2026, with unique PAN-deduplicated investors estimated at approximately 13–13.6 crore as of late 2025 into 2026.
	•	New account formation has continued at a rapid clip through 2026, with market commentary citing roughly one lakh new demat accounts opened per day during peak IPO-driven periods.
	•	A material share of this base remains inactive by transaction activity — independent analysis places dormant accounts as high as 77% of the total — indicating that account-opening has materially outpaced ongoing, informed portfolio management.
	•	SEBI has simultaneously been active on market-structure reform through 2026 (T+0 settlement, instant payout, simplified nomination, unified direct-versus-regular cost disclosure), lowering the operational friction of investing at the same time that analytical friction remains high.
The combination — a rapidly growing, increasingly digitally-native investor base, falling operational friction, and persistently low analytical/advisory penetration — is the specific gap Nxance's Portfolio Health Check and Construction engines are built to close first, before the same architecture is extended to other markets.
1.3 Why Now
Three developments converge to make this the right moment to build Nxance. First, large language models have matured to the point where a natural-language explanation layer is viable, but only if paired with a control architecture — such as Number Guard — that prevents the well-documented hallucination failure mode from reaching a financial user. Second, cloud infrastructure and managed data platforms (the FastAPI, Supabase and Railway stack Nxance currently builds on, migrating toward a Python/Rust/Go/TypeScript production architecture) make it possible for a small founding team to build institutional-grade quantitative infrastructure without institutional-scale capital. Third, regulatory frameworks in India (SEBI-RIA) and internationally are actively being modernised for digital advisory and open-finance models, including the United States' Executive Order 14405 in May 2026 directing federal regulators to streamline fintech integration with traditional financial institutions — signalling that the regulatory environment for AI-assisted financial platforms is moving toward accommodation rather than restriction across both emerging and developed markets.

5. Product Architecture
Nxance is built around three engines that function as a single collective-decision system rather than three independent tools. No single engine, and no individual AI-generated parameter inside NxanceLM, is permitted to make a decision alone — every user-facing conclusion is the product of multiple sub-engines cross-checked against each other, a design principle the team calls the Collective Decision Principle.
5.1 Portfolio Health Check Engine
The Health Check engine diagnoses a user's existing portfolio — across equities, mutual funds, bonds, fixed deposits, commodities, cryptocurrency, real estate, and other asset classes — through a 12-layer quantitative analysis framework, drawing on a wider architecture of 26 specialised sub-engines:
	•	Return analysis — XIRR computed via Newton-Raphson iteration, benchmarked against four personalised comparison points
	•	Risk metrics — Conditional Value at Risk, Value at Risk, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, and maximum drawdown
	•	Diversification analysis — Jaccard similarity across holdings to detect concealed overlap
	•	Cost analysis — expense-ratio drag quantified in exact currency terms, not a percentage abstraction
	•	Goal-alignment probability — Monte Carlo simulation across 10,000 market paths
	•	Macro sensitivity — Hidden Markov Model regime detection across ten India and global macroeconomic indicators
	•	News sentiment — FinBERT natural-language processing applied to the preceding 30 days of financial news
	•	Fraud and governance screening — cross-referenced against the SEBI enforcement database and governance red-flags
	•	Liquidity assessment, concentration risk (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index), technical entry analysis, and behavioural-profile alignment using Prospect Theory, comparing stated versus revealed risk tolerance from transaction history
The engine's output is a 0–100 portfolio health score, with every contributing issue explained in plain language and quantified in exact rupee terms. The user retains the decision — the engine identifies and explains, it does not execute.
5.2 Portfolio Construction Engine
The Construction engine builds a new portfolio from a goal-driven questionnaire — covering goals, time horizon, monthly investment capacity, and risk comfort — combined with real-time market data and Mean-Variance Optimisation using a Black-Litterman model with Ledoit-Wolf covariance shrinkage. The engine shares a 16-layer Selection Engine with the Health Check engine and, like Health Check, spans 26 finalised sub-engines. At the asset-selection step, the engine presents multiple viable options rather than a single rigid answer, builds two to three complete candidate portfolios for direct comparison, and explains which option is primary, which is secondary, and why — deliberately leading with one clearly recommended choice to avoid decision-paralysis, with alternatives available on request rather than shown flat and undifferentiated.
5.3 NxanceLM — Nxance's AI Layer, Built on a Third-Party Model
NxanceLM is the AI layer woven through both other engines as an active participant — performing research and analysis that feeds both Health Check and Construction, generating every report card and explanation the user sees, tracking user behaviour, and answering finance and economy questions directly — rather than sitting on top as a passive explainer layer. Its underlying language model is a licensed, commercially available large language model accessed via API; Nxance does not build, train, or fine-tune a language model of its own. What Nxance builds and trains in-house is the orchestration and control layer around that model — the 18 finalised mini-engines that handle retrieval, number verification, explanation generation, chat, and reporting — together with the two fully proprietary quantitative engines, Health Check and Construction, and the terminal application itself, all of which are built and calibrated entirely by Nxance. NxanceLM's intended domain scope is broad within finance and economics: it is built to work across everything number-driven within finance — investments, accounting, GDP, economy, and taxation — and is designed to be useful to both individual investors and institutions such as startup-investment firms, while remaining strictly finance/economy-domain rather than a general-purpose assistant.
5.4 NxanceLM Terminal
NxanceLM's four core layers — a Generative Reasoning Processor, a Terminal Speed Node, a Global Market Radar, and a Deep Corporate Auditor — are each surfaced as their own visible, tappable screen inside the app, rather than existing only as backend processes. The terminal interface presents a left-hand sidebar of category tabs (Market, News, Currency, Commodities, Equities, Macro, Technology, Startups, Trade, Research, Analysis) alongside a set of suggested query prompts, with a persistent header status reading "Number Guard Active | Verified Data Environment." The Market section shows live world market performance by country and by instrument class — stocks, mutual funds, fixed deposits, cryptocurrency, currency, bonds, and commodities — with drill-down into individual securities; the News section shows live news, corporate announcements, and AGM disclosures. A free-text "Ask Nxance" query box sits beneath the live data, allowing the user to ask a specific question at any time rather than only browsing structured panels.

5.6 Questionnaire & Onboarding Design
Health Check and Construction deliberately use opposite onboarding sequences. Health Check asks the user to upload their existing portfolio first and only asks profiling questions afterward, because the product's first job is diagnosis. Construction asks its goal-and-risk questionnaire first and only then designs a portfolio, following a "value-first onboarding" principle — the user experiences Nxance demonstrating understanding of their goals before being asked to commit to a specific allocation.
5.7 Tracking Tiers
Post-implementation portfolio tracking runs on two distinct data rails, and Nxance documents the difference to the user transparently rather than presenting a uniform experience. Holdings on Nxance's own-broker rail are tracked via realtime postbacks; holdings on an external broker are tracked via Account Aggregator sync or end-of-day batch sync. The team's explicit rule is never to artificially degrade the external-broker tracking experience to make the own-broker rail look comparatively better — the tier difference is disclosed as a factual limitation, not used as a growth lever.

6. Technical Research & AI System Architecture
6.1 Number Guard: The Anti-Hallucination Control Layer
The single most important architectural decision in Nxance's technical design is the Computed-Values Ledger and its enforcement layer, Number Guard. Every number shown to a user must trace to a specific engine identifier and version, a specific formula or model version, and a specific input-data snapshot; any number NxanceLM attempts to surface that does not match an entry in this ledger is blocked and regenerated rather than shown. This makes hallucinated financial figures structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely — NxanceLM's role is explicitly limited to translating already-computed figures into plain-language explanation, in English and Hindi, never to generating its own figures.

6.6 AI Model Approach
NxanceLM's underlying model is a licensed, commercially available large language model accessed via API and wrapped inside the Number Guard trust layer; Nxance's confirmed strategy is to use this third-party model rather than build, train, or fine-tune a language model of its own, on the basis that doing so is materially more capital- and resource-efficient than a from-scratch build. Nxance's proprietary technical investment is directed instead at the two quantitative engines it builds and calibrates entirely in-house — Health Check and Construction — implemented in Python and Rust with no dependency on any AI model, together with the NxanceLM orchestration layer (retrieval, number verification, explanation generation, chat, and reporting) and the terminal application itself. This is where the company's defensible technical moat sits: proprietary deterministic engines, a proprietary dataset of real portfolios and validated decisions, and the Number Guard verification layer — not the underlying language model, which is treated as a replaceable, licensed component.
6.7 Technical Risk Register
The architecture specification maintains a fourteen-item technical risk register, each with a named primary control. Representative risks include large-language-model hallucination (controlled by Number Guard), input-data quality failures, architecture complexity, the risk of quantum-computing marketing claims outrunning actual evidence, regulatory breach, model calibration drift, security incidents, latency targets being missed, data-vendor cost and licensing exposure, dependency risk on the third-party language model vendor, potential harm from behavioural-model misapplication, audit non-reproducibility, herd effects arising from any future peer-intelligence feature, and execution-flow failure modes once any execution capability is eventually built.

13. Conclusion
Nxance is built on a specific, defensible thesis: that the choice between scale and explainability in investment intelligence is a false one, provided the explanation layer is architecturally constrained to only ever describe what a deterministic engine has actually computed. That constraint — Number Guard — combined with a 26-engine Health Check framework, a 26-engine Construction framework sharing a common Selection Engine, and an 18-mini-engine orchestration layer around a licensed third-party language model, is the technical core of the company's differentiation. Nxance's own proprietary intellectual property sits in its two quantitative engines, its orchestration and verification layer, and the terminal application built around them — not in the underlying language model itself, which is deliberately treated as a replaceable, licensed component rather than something the company needs to build from scratch.
The market opportunity is real and growing on both sides of the equation the company is built to serve: a rapidly expanding, increasingly digitally-native retail investor base in India and other emerging economies with persistently low advisory penetration, and a global WealthTech software market that independent research places in the tens of billions of dollars today, trending toward materially larger figures by the early 2030s as AI-native, cloud-based platforms displace legacy infrastructure across both emerging and developed economies.
Nxance's near-term funding ask of approximately ₹12.49 crore is scoped to fund eighteen months of disciplined, phase-gated execution against an already-detailed technical and regulatory roadmap — building the backend the company has designed but not yet built, converting a proven frontend prototype and a 500-person waitlist into a live, paying product, and establishing the compliance foundation (principally SEBI-RIA registration) required to expand from suggestion-only intelligence toward personalised advice and, eventually, consent-based execution. The company's positioning as an intelligence platform — not an advisory platform — is intended to remain constant throughout this expansion, as the defining characteristic of the product rather than a stage the company will eventually outgrow.
